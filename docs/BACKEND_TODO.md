## Decisions

- 2026-10-16, "today" (live dashboard §13.3, Rate Manager money today §13.12, and later every "today" figure): the day in the **platform time zone** (`ReferenceData.platformTimezone`, UTC by default, an admin setting later), not the UTC day. Partner reports keep their own time zone choice (§13.31). Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.
- 2026-10-16, DLR threshold on the Route Rules grid (§15.4 "amber when below threshold"): for now a DLR target **per rule or per destination** (option 2). A rule's own target wins; otherwise the lowest target among the destinations it lists, where a network without its own target uses its country's. No target = no colour; block rules have none. Later, when traffic alerts exist, the alert DLR thresholds per trunk (§13.6) take over (option 3). Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.
- 2026-10-16, platform currency: one platform currency for every converted rate, margin, revenue condition (§13.9) and cross-company total, **EUR by default** and configurable (the mock reads `PLATFORM_CURRENCY`). Rate tables, invoices and SOAs keep their own currencies; a business company's base currency stays for its own billing. Screens read the currency from `ReferenceData.platformCurrency` and `lookupCurrency`, never assume one. Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.
- 2026-10-16, rule targets (§13.2, option A): a translation or HLR rule keeps what it applies to (specific trunks, a whole enterprise, a product category or all customer / all vendor trunks). A trunk added later to that enterprise or category gets the rule automatically, and its Rules tab shows it as inherited. Order and on/off stay per trunk. Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.
- 2026-10-16, MCC/MNC master (§6.1): production uses a list Hasan Ibrahim supplies as an Excel/CSV file, loaded through the MCCMNC Unique Codes import. The public list in `mock/data/` is for fake data only. Backend: the import must match existing codes, add new ones and report codes that disappear but are still used by rate lines or rules. Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.
- 2026-10-16, dial-code Master Table / numbering plan (§6.1): production uses a ready list supplied by Hasan Ibrahim and loaded through the Master Table import. The generated prefixes are fake-data only. Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.
- 2026-10-16, shared MCCs (e.g. Guam on 310/311): no special rule in the system; the supplied MCC/MNC list decides which country-level codes exist. Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.
- 2026-10-16, re-routed messages: the customer is billed once, on the final attempt; the vendor is billed for every attempt it accepted (per its billing type). Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.
- 2026-10-16, billing data retention (§13.29): EDRs, invoices, SOAs, payments, credit notes, balance history, disputes, rate history and audit records are kept for 7 years and cannot be purged before that (anonymising a closed client's contact details stays allowed, as §13.29 says). Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.
- 2026-10-16, excluded payments (§13.33): a payment with "Include in SOA" unticked is left off the partner's SOA (and shown in the SOA preview's collapsed "excluded" section) but still counts in the partner's balance and in finance reports. Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.
- 2026-10-16, best buy (§13.12): a sell rate is compared only with vendor trunks of the same product category (a DIRECT sell table against DIRECT vendors). Destinations no vendor of that category covers show no best buy and no margin. Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.
- 2026-10-16, trunk delete (§13.29): a trunk delete is blocked only by live binds. Balance, unpaid or draft invoices and open disputes belong to the enterprise and block only the enterprise delete; an open enterprise balance does not block deleting one of its trunks. Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.
- 2026-10-16, saved filter views (§13.25): a saved view is private to the user who saved it. The owner can share it with named colleagues (chosen users), who see it read-only in their view list. No department-wide or company-wide sharing. In the contract since v0.2.0 (`/views`). Decided by: Hasan Ibrahim.

## App shell notes (build step 3)

What the Phoenix backend must match so the screens need no change:

- **List queries** (`POST /{list}/query`): the mock's engine in `mock/src/lists/engine.ts` is the reference for semantics. Text operators are case-insensitive (`ILIKE`, `lower()`); `like` uses `%` / `_` with backslash escape; `regex` is POSIX case-insensitive (`~*`), and a pattern PostgreSQL rejects answers 400; negative operators (`neq`, `not_like`, `not_in`) keep rows whose value is empty; every sort ends with the record ID as tie-breaker; quick search matches the list's text columns. The mock's cursor is an offset; the backend may use keyset paging as long as the cursor stays opaque.
- **Field catalogues** (`GET /meta/lists/{list}/fields`) drive the grid's column filters and the filter builder; adding a field to a list needs no screen change.
- **Undo** (`POST /changes/{id}/undo`): restores only the fields the change touched; a later change by another user to the same record and field (not itself undone) is a conflict; undo of `create` archives the record, undo of `delete` restores it; the undo is a new change with `undo_of_id`; a change can be undone once (400 afterwards); undoing someone else's change needs the `undo_others` permission (403 otherwise).
- **Live socket**: `/socket/websocket?vsn=2.0.0`, Phoenix Channels V2 frames. The shell joins `live:tps` (tick once a second) and `alerts:<userId>` (incidents). The browser opens one socket in the main window and relays it to tabs dragged into other windows.
- **Workspace**: the GUI saves the open tabs (path, title, pinned) about a second after each change; page state (filters, sort, columns) is inside each tab's path.

## Enterprise and trunk notes (build step 4.1)

- **Saves** answer `{record, change}`: the saved record plus the change it recorded (null when nothing differed), so the screen shows "Saved · change #n" with Undo (§13.10). The change diff is field-level with API field paths (`address.city`, `contacts.finance.emails`, `billing.customer.creditLimit`, `rateTableIds`, `rules.translation`, `accountManagers`, `archived`). Undo must be able to put each of these back; the mock's appliers are `applyEnterpriseField` (`mock/src/api/enterprises.ts`) and `applyTrunkField` (`mock/src/api/trunks.ts`).
- **Passwords** are write-only: never returned (`passwordSet` instead), never written to the change log (the diff says `(changed)`), so undo can't restore an old password (§13.22).
- **Reserved names** (§13.29): enterprise names and trunk names are unique per kind including archived records, and customer trunk usernames (our SMPP system_id) are unique including archived ones. Vendor trunk usernames are the vendor's credentials and may repeat.
- **Safe delete** blockers: enterprise = live binds, unpaid or draft invoices, non-zero customer or vendor balance, open disputes (partner invoices in mismatch / pending); trunk = live binds. Info items: trunks archived with the enterprise, route rules, rate tables, rules attached directly, traffic in the last 30 days (the mock answers `count: null`; the backend reads the EDR store). An override needs an administrator and a written reason, and the reason is the change note. Archiving an enterprise archives its live trunks in the same change; restore (admin only) brings back the trunks archived with it.
- **Balance** (§5.3): customer = opening balance + invoice usage charges − credits + debits; vendor = opening balance + accepted / matched partner invoices − payments (debits) + credits. Excluded payments still count (§13.33).
- **Handover** (§13.30): effective now or later, never in the past; a new handover replaces a scheduled one that hasn't started; the current AM is the history row covering now. The mock applies due handovers when the record is read; the backend should run this as a job at the effective time. Undo after the effective time should need a manager (not enforced by the mock yet).
- **Trunk Rules tab** (§13.2): shows every translation (and, for customer trunks, HLR) rule that reaches the trunk, with where it comes from (the trunk, its enterprise, its category, all trunks), in run order (same as `rulesForTrunk`). Saving a list gives the listed rules positions 1..n; other positioned rules follow. Detach removes only the rule's direct trunk target.
- **Vendor trunks** get their own buy rate table on create (vendor billing currency, else USD) (§13.12). A trunk can't move to another enterprise; clone it instead.
- **Docs** are stored in the database in this phase (max 10 MB, base64 upload); the backend may use object storage behind the same endpoints.

## Rate Manager notes (build step 4.2)

- **Versions, never edits in place** (§6.2, §13.29): a new rate from `effectiveFrom` closes the version valid then (sets `valid_to`) and opens a new one; versions starting at or after that date are replaced. `effectiveFrom` can't be in the past (one minute of clock skew allowed), so traffic already sent keeps its rate (§13.32). Closing a scheduled line withdraws it and the version before it runs on as long as the withdrawn one would have. Blocking flags the line in place.
- **Change log** for rate edits: entity `rate_table`, record id = the table, one diff entry per touched line with field `line` and full line snapshots before/after (null = the line didn't exist). Undo deletes, then updates, then inserts, so no two versions of a destination overlap at any step (the exclusion constraints stay on). A line edited twice in one batch collapses to its first and last state. An undo change lists the same record ids as the change it undoes, and its entries run in reverse order.
- **Notice period** (§13.14): an increase on a customer table that starts sooner than the longest `rate_notice_days` among the clients using the table answers 409 with the breaches; `acceptNoticeBreach` applies it and the change note says so. New destinations and decreases are never a breach. Vendor tables have no check.
- **Best buy** = the cheapest rate valid now for the same MCCMNC and product category among live, active vendor trunks, without sender-ID or blocked lines, converted through the latest exchange rate (`best_buy_rates`). The backend should keep it current on every vendor rate change and when scheduled rates start; the mock refreshes it after vendor edits and every 5 minutes (about 3 s at full volume). `rate_table_stats` holds the per-table counts the list sorts on: refreshed for one table after its edits and for all after a best-buy refresh.
- **Traffic figures** (avg margin weighted by today's volume, revenue or cost today) come from the EDR store: customer tables by `c_rate_table_id`, vendor tables by `v_rate_table_id`, today in the platform time zone. They are null when the EDR store doesn't answer, and not filterable or sortable (no operators in the field catalogue).
- **Database settings**: integer list filters compare as `bigint` so they use the ID indexes (a `numeric` cast made `rateTableId = n` scan all 1.4 M lines), and JIT is off for API connections (it added 2 s of compile time to list queries with many small subqueries).
- Not in this step: rate import wizard and rate notices to clients (§12.1, §13.13; the buttons show disabled), Bulk rating opens the bulk workspace (§13.11, build step 5).

## Route Rules notes (build step 4.3)

- **Saving** (§6.3, §13.10): `PUT /route-rules/{id}` takes the whole rule; the change records only the fields that differ (vendors and whitelists as whole lists), so undo restores them. A save that changes only `status` is logged as `enable` / `disable`. The GUI saves one condition or one vendor edit at a time, so each is one undoable change.
- **Drafts**: a rule may be saved off (inactive) without vendors, so a new rule can be built step by step in the detail panel. A static rule needs at least one non-backup vendor to be on: `PUT` with `status: active` answers 400 on `/vendors`, and bulk `enable` answers 400 naming the rules. New rules from the GUI start off.
- **Evaluation order** (mock `src/routing/evaluate.ts`, for the Elixir switch to match): status and start/stop time, then category, customer trunk, destination (codes, 3-digit code = whole country, plus the MCCMNC regex), sender / receiver / content patterns and lists, HLR ported, TPS (§13.8, with tolerance for `=`), revenue (§13.9; per message from the customer rate, accumulated over its period and scope), joined by `matchMode` all / any. Then vendor choice: number whitelist → warm-up threshold → hunt policy (percentage split, top down, round robin) for static rules, cheapest first for dynamic, quality score for QBR; vendors leaving less than `margin` (flat) or the revenue condition's minimum margin are skipped, and so are vendors at `skipVendorAtTpsPct` of their TPS. The simulator tries active rules by priority (then ID) and stops at the first match unless Continue routing is YES.
- **Traffic figures** (§13.27): the grid columns read `route_rule_traffic`, refreshed from the EDR store every 60 s for the five windows (the window ends at the latest EDR, so fake data with a fixed time range still shows figures). ASR = submits accepted ÷ attempts, DLR = delivered ÷ accepted, margin = revenue − cost in the platform currency (each EDR's `*_sys` amounts are in its business company's base currency, so the mock converts them per company at query time). The backend should feed this from the live counters (Redis) instead of a periodic query. Without the EDR store the figures are null and the traffic endpoint answers `available: false`.
- **Bulk** (§13.11): one change for all rules; replace vendor swaps the trunk on every level and in whitelists. Archive is the safe delete (§13.29); a rule has no blocking dependencies.
- Not in this step: Route map, LCR, Re-rating, Rate lookup and Simulator as their own pages (build steps 4.4 and 5; the header Simulate dialog already runs `POST /route-rules/simulate`), and the predictive route costing panel while editing vendors (the Test section shows cost and margin per candidate).

## Rate Lookup notes (build step 4.4)

- **Which line counts** (§6.2, §5.4): for a network, a table's line for that MCCMNC, else its country line ("all networks"); for a whole country, its country line, else its cheapest network line (shown with `matchedCode` and a `*` in the GUI). Sender-ID lines count only when `senderId` is given. Blocked lines never count. A client's rate is the first of its trunk's rate tables (by position) that has a line, as the switch rates traffic.
- **Converted figures and margins** are in the platform currency (EUR by default, see Decisions) at the latest exchange rate: amount ÷ its currency's rate per USD × the platform currency's rate per USD. `lookupCurrency` names it. Route evaluation (revenue conditions, the margin rule, least-cost order) uses the same converted figures.
- **Routed vendor** per client and destination: today's active rules in priority order (§6.3), with the trunk's category and its sell rate as the per-message revenue; warm-up thresholds and number whitelists are left out (no receiver is known), so it is the steady-state route. A vendor is "routed" when it is the routed vendor for at least one client. The mock routes in bulk (rules pre-filtered by destination, a yes/no match test, sorted least-cost pools cached per destination, one evaluation per distinct trunk category / sell rate / sender), about 0.1 s for a destination with 400 clients. The backend should keep this next to the route cache it already builds for the switch.
- **Traffic figures**: vendor 24 h and client 7 days by `c_mccmnc` (the country: every network of it), ending at the latest EDR like the route-rule windows; null without the EDR store.
- **Highlights**: below cost (a client's sell under the best cost), cheaper than routed (the best vendor is cheaper than a client's routed vendor), expiring soon (a rate ends within 14 days with no version after it; a line closed by a scheduled change is not expiring), single vendor. Filtering on highlights computes every destination of the lookup first, so it is limited to 1,000 destinations (the backend may precompute these flags).
- **Matrix columns**: the 60 trunks covering most destinations of the lookup after filters (`columnsTruncated` when more exist); rows page 50 at a time. Timings at full volume (Australia + Germany, 59 destinations): 0.8–1.9 s per page depending on the view (routing makes vendor costs and margin the slowest); easy mode 0.08–0.6 s.
- Not in this step: account manager masking and hiding in lookups (§13.17, its own build step; the lookup already filters by account manager), "Update rates" and "Change route" from the results (the bulk workspace, §13.11, build step 5; the buttons show disabled), Excel export (CSV and clipboard copy for now).

## Product reference data notes (build step 5.1)

- **Imports** (§6.1 import modals) always run as a dry run first in the GUI; the same request with `dryRun: false` saves all or nothing, as one change that Undo reverses (rows added are archived again, rows changed get their old values, rows archived come back). Up to 200 errors, remarks and kept rows are listed. CSV may use commas or semicolons.
- **MCCMNC import, replace mode**: codes missing from the file are archived only when nothing uses them (open or scheduled rate lines, route rules, translation rules, live dial codes, MO numbers); the others are kept and listed. This is the "report codes that disappear but are still used" part of the 2026-10-16 MCC/MNC decision.
- **Safe delete** (§13.29): a product category is blocked while trunks, rate tables, route rules or translation / HLR rules use it; an MCCMNC code while rate lines, rules, dial codes or MO numbers use it (its 30-day traffic is shown, not blocking). Countries are not deleted. Restore is for administrators, as for other records.
- **Changing a code**: the MCC and MNC of a code in use cannot change (409), because rate lines, rules and dial codes refer to the code; network name, original MNC and country always can.
- **Number resolution** (Rate Lookup, route tests) uses the live prefixes of the default "All" profile only. Archived codes no longer appear in the destination search or as valid targets.
- **Fake data**: two extra profiles ("Ported numbers", "Short ranges") and 600 MO numbers (60 in the small profile), generated as the last step (`mock/src/generator/masterData.ts`) so the rest of the seed is unchanged.

## Live dashboard notes (build step 4.6)

- **Movers** (§13.3 item 2) are per client trunk: the average TPS over the last window against the window before. Started = below 0.05 TPS before and at least 0.2 TPS now; stopped = the reverse; increasing / decreasing = both windows sending and the change beyond ± the threshold. Each list is capped at 15, biggest first. The backend computes them per joined client, since window and threshold are join params; the mock keeps 120 per-minute averages per trunk for that (`computeMovers` in `mock/src/live/simulator.ts`).
- **Today** is the day in the platform time zone (`ReferenceData.platformTimezone`, `PLATFORM_TIMEZONE`, UTC by default); see Decisions.
- **Simulator** (mock only): trunk shares, revenue, cost, ASR and DLR per message come from the last 7 days of EDRs, today's totals start from ClickHouse, and each trunk follows a timeline of steady / ramp / start / stop segments. Platform volume is `SIM_TOTAL_TPS` (default 1,800).
- Not in this step: §4 server health pills (cache, CPU, memory) belong with the switch and monitoring screens (build step 5, they need the switch's metrics); "which TPS rule is active per client" (§13.8 visibility) comes with the TPS rule data in the tick once the switch reports it.

## Enterprise report notes (build step 4.5)

- **Time zone cut** (§13.31): in partner mode every trunk's traffic is cut in the trunk's billing time zone, else its enterprise's. The mock groups trunks by zone, aggregates each group in ClickHouse with `toDate(submit_time, tz)` etc. and the date range converted in that zone, then combines the groups (rows only merge across zones when no period is shown). Each row carries its `timezone`; the response lists the zones used, and the CSV prints them. UTC and platform modes use one zone. Platform time zone: `PLATFORM_TIMEZONE`, default UTC (an admin setting later).
- **Billing is by submit time** (`submit_time`). Archived trunks still count (their traffic is billed, §13.29).
- **Money**: revenue and cost "in the partner currency" are the partner's own charges (customer revenue in its rate currency, vendor cost in its currency); the other leg is converted into the partner currency through the base rate of the EDR (`c_ex_rate` / `v_ex_rate`). "(B)" columns are in the platform currency (EUR), converted per business company. Partner-currency totals are null when the rows hold several currencies.
- **Metrics**: Successful = accepted by the vendor (`is_submit_success`), Submitted = handed to a vendor, DLR (C) = delivered as reported to the customer ÷ successful, DLR (V) = vendor receipts DELIVRD ÷ successful, Reported = a final status other than ENROUTE, delay = average for delivered messages. The backend should confirm these definitions against the reference platform's report before invoices are compared (§16 "behaviour marked inferred").
- **Speed**: the mock reads the raw `edr` table (5 M rows over 30 days in the full seed). The backend should read `edr_hourly` for whole-hour zones and the raw table only for zones with a half-hour offset.
- Not in this step, comes with the Finance screens (build step 5): receiving partner invoices by email or upload, the per-partner invoice mapping, reconciliation tolerance rules (Enterprise › Billing › Reconciliation rules), the comparison and result screens, Accept / Dispute / Reject with emails, and the SOA (§13.31 "Receiving invoices" to "Manual SOA option"). The report's API is the comparison's input.

## DLR target notes

- Targets live on the MCCMNC master (`mccmnc.dlr_target_pct`) and on the rule (`route_rules.dlr_target_pct`), edited on the MCCMNC codes page (`/rates/mccmnc`) and in the rule's settings dialog. The rule list computes the effective target in SQL (`dlrTargetSql` in `mock/src/lists/defs.ts`), so "DLR below target" can be filtered on the server.
- A rule that lists no destinations (all, or an exclusion list) and has no target of its own has no target.
- Fake data: countries 55–80 %, about one network in eight with its own target, about one rule in 25 with its own (`mock/src/generator/dlrTargets.ts`, deterministic, so the rest of the seed is unchanged).
